# Plan

## Design

- A **signature** gives symbols with the number of auxiliary ports; every agent also has one
  principal port.
- A **net** has two kinds of entities, *agents* and *wires* (two ports, no principal port),
  plus an interface `ι` of free ports.
- **Ports** are an inductive type: `free i`, `pri a`, `aux a k`, `wire w s` (with
  `s : Side`).
- **Wiring** is a fixed-point-free involution `link` on ports. Free ports may only be linked
  to entity ports, so gluing nets never creates chains of free ports that would need to be
  followed.
- Because wires are explicit, `a — w — b` and `a — b` are different nets. Isomorphism stays
  strict (no wire flipping). The equivalence `≈` is *isomorphism up to wires*: a
  label-preserving bijection of agents that preserves and reflects `Connected` on terminal
  ports (free ports and agent ports). `Connected` is the equivalence relation on all ports
  generated by links and wires; its classes are paths (two terminal ports with wire ends in
  between) and cycles of wire ends. Chains of wires, wire orientation and closed loops of
  wires are invisible to `≈`.

## Milestones

0. [x] Project setup: Lake + Mathlib.
   - [ ] CI workflow (`.github/workflows/ci.yml` using `leanprover/lean-action`).
1. [x] Signatures, ports, nets, active pairs; example nets.
2. [x] Isomorphism of nets (`Equiv` on agents and on wires, preserving labels and links,
   identity on the interface); setoid.
3. [x] Gluing nets along their interfaces (`glue : Net S (ι ⊕ κ) → Net S (κ ⊕ ι') →
   Net S (ι ⊕ ι')`), interface reindexing; gluing respects iso, is associative and
   commutative up to iso.
4. [x] Connections through wires and the equivalence `≈` (isomorphism up to wires);
   reindexing and gluing respect `≈`; the identity net of wires is a unit for gluing up to
   `≈`.
5. [x] Interaction systems: the active pair `α ⋈ β` as a net (`activePair`, with
   `(α ⋈ β).swap ≅ β ⋈ α`); rules for pairs of symbols (RHS net with interface
   `Fin (arity α) ⊕ Fin (arity β)`), at most one rule per pair (`rule α β` is an `Option`),
   symmetry up to `≈` (the rule for `β ⋈ α` is the rule for `α ⋈ β` with its interface
   swapped). Example: the rules for unary addition.
6. [x] Reduction: `I.Step N N'` iff `N ≈ C.plug (α ⋈ β)` and `N' ≈ C.plug rhs`, where
   `C.plug R` glues `R` into the hole `κ` of a context `C : Net S (ι ⊕ κ)`; multi-step
   reduction `I.Reduces` (`Relation.ReflTransGen` of `Step`, followed by `≈`, so that zero
   steps relate equivalent nets); normal forms `I.Normal`. Redexes are exactly the active pairs
   up to wires (`IsActivePairW`: principal ports connected through wires) whose symbols have a
   rule (`exists_step_iff`). The hard direction cuts an active pair out of its net
   (`removePair`, `equiv_plug_removePair`).
7. [x] Metatheory: strong confluence modulo `≈` (`Step.strong_confluence`: the two steps
   reduce the same active pair, the same pair from the other side, or disjoint pairs);
   Church–Rosser (`Reduces.church_rosser`, via `Relation.church_rosser` on nets up to `≈`),
   uniqueness of normal forms (`Reduces.normal_unique`), equal length of normalising
   reductions (`ReducesIn.length_eq`), and no reduction is longer than a normalising one
   (`ReducesIn.le_of_normal`). The proof rests on contexts being determined by plugging in an
   active pair (`WireIso.ofPlugActivePair`) and on exchanging the two holes of a context
   (`plugExchange`).
8. [x] More examples: unary arithmetic (`Z`, `S`, `add`, `mul`, eraser and duplicator for
   numerals); the interaction combinators `γ, δ, ε` (`Combinators.system`, every pair has a
   rule), with a complete three-step reduction to normal form. General lemmas added along the
   way: `step_activePair` (an active pair reduces to its rule), `Step.plug`/`Reduces.plug`
   (reduction is closed under contexts, via `plugPlug`).

## Possible extensions

- Interaction calculus (term syntax) and its correspondence with the graph semantics.
- Executable reducer proven correct against the reduction relation.
- Universality of interaction combinators.
- Encodings of λ-calculus / linear logic.
